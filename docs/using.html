Using coCine

One of you puts a film on; everybody else gets it from them and watches it at the same time. This page is everything you need to run a film night, and nothing you don't.

Installing it

Your computerDownloadWhat to do
Windows coCine-x64.exe Double-click it and follow the installer. Windows may show a blue “Windows protected your PC” box the first time — click More info, then Run anyway. That appears because the app is not yet signed with a paid certificate, not because anything is wrong with it.
Ubuntu, Mint, Debian coCine-amd64.deb Double-click it and let the software installer do the rest. This is the option to pick if you have a choice: it installs the video player coCine needs at the same time.
Other Linux coCine-x86_64.AppImage Right-click → Properties → tick “allow executing as a program”, then double-click. You also need mpv installed; if it is missing, coCine tells you the exact one-line command for your system and gives you a button to copy it.
macOS — Not yet. The Mac version has to be built on a Mac, and that has not happened.
What coCine needs from you

A film file on your computer if you are the one putting it on — coCine plays files, it does not stream from Netflix or anywhere else. Everyone else needs nothing except the app and the room code.

The first time you open it

Two boxes on the right, and then you are done for good — coCine remembers both.

  • Your name. What everyone else sees. Anything you like.
  • Server. A meeting point. It carries chat, keeps everyone's clocks in step and introduces people to each other — it never receives the film. If whoever invited you sent an address, paste it here. If the app came with one already filled in, leave it alone.
If the Server box says 127.0.0.1

That address means “this computer”, which is a placeholder rather than a real meeting point. Ask whoever invited you for the address they use, and paste it in. There is a use default button to get back to the one the app shipped with.

Hosting a film night

  1. Create the room first. Check the three settings in Your room (explained below, and all changeable later), then press Create a room. A film is always watched with somebody, so the room comes first — Open film stays greyed out until there is one.
  2. Send the code. A code like BCDF-GHJK appears at the top. Click it to copy, and send it to whoever is watching. That code is the only thing anybody needs.
  3. Put a film on. Press Open film and pick the file, or drag it onto the window. It plays on your machine only at this point. Nothing has left your computer, and nobody else can see it yet — so this is the moment to check you have the right file, the right subtitles, the right everything.
  4. Press Start sharing. Now the film goes to the room. A panel appears showing everybody's progress: how much each person has, how fast, who is furthest behind, and which parts of the film they hold.
  5. Press play when the room is ready. Everyone starts at the same moment.
Opening and sharing are separate on purpose

They used to be one action, which made a small decision (watch this file) and a large one (send several gigabytes to three people) look identical. Now nothing leaves your machine until you say so.

While you are sharing

  • Pause sharing stops your machine feeding the room — useful if you need the bandwidth for something else. It pauses for everybody: whoever was downloading from you stops until you resume. The panel says so plainly, and other people see you marked paused.
  • Unload film closes the film so you can open a different one. If you were the one sharing it, it comes off the room too, so nobody is left waiting for bytes that are not coming.
You do not have to wait for the download to finish

coCine only waits until everybody has a decent head start — typically a few per cent of the film. The rest keeps arriving while you all watch.

Watching the transfer

Once a film is being shared, the sidebar shows what is actually happening — the numbers the person sharing needs and could not otherwise see:

  • Your own line — upload rate, download rate, and how many people you are connected to.
  • A row per person — how much of the film they hold, their download and upload rates, and whether they are keeping up. Whoever is furthest behind is named.
  • A bar showing which parts they have, from the start of the film on the left to the end on the right. Two people can both be at 50% with one of them about to run out and the other fine; this is what tells them apart.
  • Whether it is safe for you to leave — that is, whether a second complete copy exists in the room yet.

Joining someone else's

  1. Open coCine and check your name is right.
  2. Paste the code into the CODE box and press Join.
  3. Once the host presses Start sharing, the film starts arriving on its own. You will see it counting up. Before that they may be watching it locally — nothing is wrong.
  4. Wait for the host to press play. You do not need to press anything.

You can arrive late. If the host has left Pause when someone arrives late on, the room pauses so you can catch up; otherwise the film carries on and you join wherever everybody else is.

While you are watching

  • Play, pause and seek affect everybody. That is the point. If the host has given you control, your pause pauses the room.
  • The number at the top (something like 38 ms) is how far your screen is from everyone else's. Green is good. It fixes itself constantly by running a fraction faster or slower, which you will not notice.
  • Fullscreen with the button at the bottom right or F. Esc comes back.

Chat and voice

Chat

The panel on the right. It also records what happened — who joined, who put a film on, who took control — so there is one place to look when you wonder what just happened.

In fullscreen, press Enter and type — the line appears over the bottom of the picture as you write it. Enter again sends it, Esc throws it away and puts the keyboard back on playback. Messages appear as small bubbles over the bottom-left of the picture and fade away after a few seconds; nothing else covers the film.

Voice

  • Press Join voice. Your microphone stays shut until you hold V.
  • Wear headphones. On speakers, your microphone picks up the film and sends it back to everyone. Push-to-talk keeps that survivable, and the film quietens while you talk, but headphones are the real fix.
  • mute stops you being heard; deafen also stops you hearing the room.
  • The host can ask you to mute. Your app does it for you — but it is a request, not something the host can enforce, and coCine says so rather than pretending otherwise.

Sending the film: the one real choice

When you create a room you pick how the film gets to everybody. Both options end with the same film on the same screens; they differ in who carries it.

Between us — peer to peer

The default, and usually right. The film goes straight from your computer to your friends', and each of them starts passing on the parts they already have. Nothing is stored anywhere else, and it costs nobody anything.

Gets faster with more people, not slower — after the first few minutes they are largely feeding each other.

Through the server — relay

You upload the film once to the server's storage, and everybody downloads it from there. Nobody has to be able to reach anybody else, and you can close your laptop once the upload is done.

Costs whoever pays for the server, and you wait for the upload before anyone can start.

Which one should I pick?

If…PickWhy
You have no idea Between us It works for most people, costs nothing, and you can switch if it doesn't.
You are all on home broadband or wifi Between us This is exactly the case it is built for.
Someone is on mobile data or a phone hotspot Through the server Mobile networks usually stop two people connecting to each other directly. Voice will still work; the film is the part that struggles.
Someone is on office, campus or hotel wifi Through the server Those networks often block the kind of direct connection the film needs.
Your upload speed is very slow and there are several of you Through the server You upload once instead of feeding everybody, and after that your connection stops being the bottleneck.
You want to start the film and then close your laptop Through the server Peer to peer needs somebody who has the film to stay in the room until a second person has all of it.
You tried “between us” and somebody is stuck at 0% Through the server That is the sign a direct connection could not be made. Switching re-shares the film, so allow time for the upload.
Telling them apart while you watch

If everyone can hear each other perfectly but somebody's film is not moving, that is not a broken app — voice and film travel differently, and voice can get through where the film cannot. That is the moment to switch to Through the server.

The relay option only appears if the server you are using has storage set up for it. If it is missing, whoever runs the server has not turned it on.

What the host can do

SettingWhat it means
Who can control playback Everyone, or only you. Change it at any time with the Anyone who joins can control playback tick box, or per person by hovering their name and pressing give control / take control.
Pause when someone arrives late On: the film waits for the newcomer. Off: it carries on.
Start without… Begins even though somebody has not received enough of the film. It names who gets left behind, because that is the decision you are making.
make host Hands the room over. Useful when you have to leave and everyone else is staying.
ask to mute Asks somebody's app to mute them. Advisory, as above.

If the host leaves, the room does not collapse — whoever has been there longest becomes host automatically.

Films kept on your computer

Films you receive are kept, so watching one again does not download it again. Press Films in the top bar to see what is stored, how much space it uses and how much you have left, and to delete anything you are done with. A film that never finished downloading is marked partial.

Nothing is deleted automatically. Films can be several gigabytes each, so it is worth a look occasionally.

When something goes wrong

“Open film” doesn't seem to do anything

Fixed, and worth knowing what it was: on some Linux desktops the system's own file chooser told coCine you had cancelled whenever you double-clicked a file. coCine now uses its own file browser on Linux, so double-click, Enter and the Open film button all work. If you are on an older copy, update it.

The picture goes black when I go fullscreen

The sound keeps playing and the picture never comes back, even after leaving fullscreen and returning. This was mpv choosing a video output that cannot survive its window being resized, and coCine now asks for a different one on Linux. If you still see it, start coCine from a terminal with an output of your own choosing: 

COCINE_MPV_ARGS="--vo=x11" cocine

--vo=xv and --gpu-sw=yes are the other two worth trying. If one of them fixes it, that is worth reporting, along with the graphics card you have.

It says “reconnecting”

Your connection to the meeting point dropped — wifi blip, or the server restarted. It keeps trying by itself and rejoins the same room. If it never comes back, the server is down; nothing on your side will fix that.

Somebody is stuck at 0%, but chat works

Their computer and yours cannot reach each other directly. Chat and voice go through the meeting point, so they keep working. Switch the room to Through the server — see above.

Everyone hears an echo of the film

Somebody is on speakers. Headphones fix it completely; there is no setting that can, because the echo remover cannot hear the film coCine is playing.

“The film needs the sharer for now”

Only one complete copy exists — the host's. If they leave, the rest of the download stops. Once a second person has the whole film it changes to safe for the sharer to leave.

coCine says it needs mpv

mpv is the video player coCine drives. On the screen you are looking at there is a command and a Copy the command button: paste it into a terminal, press enter, then start coCine again. On Ubuntu-like systems, installing the .deb instead avoids this entirely.

Still stuck? Open an issue — say what you saw and what you expected, and it helps enormously to say which of the two sharing options the room was using.
