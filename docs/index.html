What it actually does

Everyone sees the same frame

Nobody is ever told to “play now”. The room agrees on where the film should be at a given moment, and every machine works out the rest for itself, correcting by changing speed a fraction of a percent rather than jumping.

Measured: 35 ms at the 99th percentile across five clients on a simulated 40 ms link.

The film comes from whoever has it

The person who opens the film shares it directly with the room, and everyone who receives a piece starts sharing it too. Watching begins once there is enough of a head start — usually long before the download finishes.

Measured: watchable with 2.9% of the film in hand.

Talk over it

Voice runs directly between people, and chat doubles as the room's log. In fullscreen, messages float over the picture and cover nothing else.

One person is in charge, gently

The host decides who can drive playback, whether the room waits for latecomers, and whether to start before somebody slow has caught up. Everything else is shared.

Where the project is

Built and working: player control, the synchronisation engine, rooms and roles, transfer, voice, relay fallbacks, and installers for Windows and Linux. Honest about the rest — the peer-to-peer parts have been measured extensively on one machine, and the thing that still needs proving is two real machines on two real home networks.

The full list of what is deferred, blocked or known-broken lives in docs/TODO.md, and the short version is on the contributing page.
